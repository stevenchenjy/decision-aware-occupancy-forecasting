\section{Introduction}

Building energy-flexibility research considers how operational demand can be adjusted while respecting service constraints~\cite{Chen2018,Junker2018}. Occupancy is an important contextual signal for that task: it can identify intervals in which a building zone may be empty and, therefore, potentially suitable for a carefully constrained operational action. Occupancy modeling has long been studied for building energy management~\cite{Erickson2014}, and recent reviews show a broad forecasting literature spanning data acquisition, estimation, prediction, and evaluation~\cite{Jin2021,CaballeroPena2024,Li2024}. Yet a good occupancy forecast alone does not answer whether a recommendation can be made at a useful time, for a useful duration, and at an acceptable observed conflict rate.

That distinction matters because the outcome appropriate to a forecasting study is not automatically an energy outcome. Occupancy-prediction-based cooling-control studies and HVAC-control evaluations can assess a modeled or field operating intervention~\cite{Peng2018,EsrafilianNajafabadi2022,Lin2023}. Such evidence is stronger than a retrospective coincidence between a score, a camera-derived Empty label, and a processed meter-derived load proxy. This paper deliberately studies the latter: an \emph{offline safe load opportunity}. It sums the HVAC-plus-lighting proxy during intervals that a fixed rule recommends and that are subsequently camera-label-empty. It is neither a counterfactual estimate of energy saved nor a claim that the same load can be shifted without comfort, equipment, or control constraints.

The analysis also makes an otherwise easy-to-miss timing distinction explicit. The repository's 15-min records are left-labelled. A row labelled 00:00 summarizes the input bin $[00{:}00,00{:}15)$ and is therefore treated here as available only at 00:15. This converts the existing evidence into a retrospective, post-bin next-record analysis; it cannot support a claim that a recommendation was available at 00:00. The upstream input is a cleaned release, and neither original raw streams nor source-side imputation provenance are committed. Thus, post-import row ordering is checked, but fully prospective data availability is not established.

The bounded research question is: \emph{for one office-building time series, how do validation-selected Empty-class scores trade off forecast discrimination, subsequently observed camera-label conflict, and offline safe load opportunity when recommendations must form at least one-hour stable windows?} The evaluation separates two scopes that are often conflated. Forecast discrimination is measured on all overlapping rolling 24-h forecasts. In contrast, the operating rule is selected and evaluated only on non-overlapping midnight-labelled daily horizons, so policy accounting does not double count repeated predictions for the same interval.

The paper makes three contributions. First, it documents an explicit post-bin chronological protocol that maps 24-h Empty-class scores to stable-window recommendations without using controllable loads as predictor inputs. Second, it compares a historical schedule baseline, LightGBM, and a validation-selected score blend using both AUPRC and a validation-constrained offline policy outcome. Third, it exposes the uncertainty and boundary conditions behind the point estimates: the selected blend has a slightly higher AUPRC than the two baselines and zero observed camera-label conflicts in the held-out policy period, but paired test contrasts cross zero and validation perturbations do not identify unique weights or a unique threshold. The contribution is an evaluative saved-output case study, not a deployed controller, a real-time decision system, or a decision-focused learning algorithm.

Section~II positions this evaluation against occupancy forecasting and control research. Sections~III and~IV define the data, timing, policy, and experimental protocol. Section~V reports canonical results, and Sections~VI--VII discuss their limits and implications. Appendix~A records the status of the expanded decision-aware and window-aware searches without using them to support the primary conclusion.
