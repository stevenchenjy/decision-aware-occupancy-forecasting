Occupancy forecasts are useful for building operations only when their information timing and downstream decision are made explicit. This paper analyzes saved outputs from a single-building, offline \emph{post-bin} case study at 15-min resolution. The historical schedule baseline, LightGBM, and a compact Transformer encoder produce uncalibrated Empty-class scores; their convex blend is selected on validation AUPRC, and its score threshold is selected separately on validation to maximize offline safe load opportunity subject to an empirical interval conflict cutoff and a one-hour stable-window rule. The stored anchor label $t$ denotes the left label of a completed input bin $[t,t+15\text{ min})$, so its effective availability boundary is $t+15$ min. Accordingly, the policy's midnight-labelled anchors are not claims of recommendations available at 00:00. Across 388,032 overlapping held-out forecast rows, the selected blend has Empty AUPRC 0.8514, versus 0.8497 for the historical baseline and 0.8382 for LightGBM. Across 43 non-overlapping midnight-labelled policy horizons, whose effective boundary is 00:15, its fixed rule recommends 259 intervals; all are subsequently camera-label-empty and form 14 label-safe windows, coinciding with 490.1~kWh of processed HVAC-plus-lighting load proxy. LightGBM coincides with 493.9~kWh but has 11 camera-label conflicts. Validation perturbations show that neither the blend weights nor the threshold is uniquely stable. The result is therefore evidence about reproducible saved-output, post-bin opportunity accounting---not verified energy savings, comfort, physical absence, calibrated risk, real-time availability, or controller performance. Source-side imputation provenance and raw-stream timestamps remain unresolved; a prospective empirical rerun is required for operational claims.
