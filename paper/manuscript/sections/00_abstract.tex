Building operations need occupancy forecasts that arrive in time, last long enough, and rarely conflict with later occupancy observations. We study this problem in a single-building, 15-min, offline \emph{post-bin} case study. The primary \emph{hybrid} is a weighted average of Empty-class scores from a historical schedule, LightGBM, and a compact Transformer, combining recurring, nonlinear, and sequential patterns. Validation area under the precision--recall curve (AUPRC) selects the weights. AUPRC summarizes Empty-class precision and recall across score thresholds; higher values indicate better discrimination when class frequencies are unequal. A separate validation search selects the operating threshold that maximizes a load-opportunity proxy under a 10\% conflict-rate limit and a one-hour minimum window. Conflict rate is the share of recommended intervals later labelled occupied. The opportunity proxy sums processed HVAC-plus-lighting load in recommendations later labelled Empty and represents load available for further operational investigation. A stored anchor $t$ covers the completed input bin $[t,t+15\text{ min})$, making the forecast available at $t+15$ min. Across 388,032 overlapping held-out forecast rows, hybrid AUPRC is 0.8514, compared with 0.8497 for the historical baseline and 0.8382 for LightGBM. Across 43 non-overlapping policy horizons, the fixed hybrid policy recommends 259 intervals; all are subsequently camera-label-empty, form 14 label-safe windows, and coincide with 490.1~kWh of processed load proxy. LightGBM finds a similar opportunity (493.9~kWh) with 11 conflicts, whereas the conflict-free historical policy finds 94.6~kWh. The hybrid therefore preserves strong forecast discrimination while improving the observed opportunity--conflict trade-off. A prospective rerun with verified source timestamps is the next step toward operational evaluation.
